Usage & history

Ukrainian is the native language of around 40 million people and is written using a version of the Cyrillic script ( українська абетка ʊkrɐˈjinʲsʲkɐ ɐˈbɛtkɐ Ukrainian alphabet ). Although Ukraine has many Russian speakers, particularly in the east and south of the country, in 2001 around 88% of Ukrainians were able to communicate in the Ukrainian language, and its use in the population has been increasing for some years.wul

The script is named in honor of the two Byzantine brothers, Saints Cyril and Methodius, who created the earlier Glagolitic alphabet. Modern scholars believe that Cyrillic was developed and formalized by early disciples of Cyril and Methodius.

Cyrillic is derived from the Greek uncial script, augmented by letters from the older Glagolitic alphabet, including some ligatures. These additional letters were used for Old Church Slavonic sounds not found in Greek, and the Cyrillic script was initially used for writing Old Church Slavonic (also called Old Bulgarian), The script has changed over the intervening centuries to the point that Old Church Slavonic is sometimes considered a separate script.

Several orthographic reforms were introduced over the course of the 19th and 20th centuries. The latest version of the orthography was defined by the Ukrainian National Commission on Spelling and approved by the Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine on May 22, 2019.

Basic features

The Cyrillic script is an alphabet, ie. all vowels are written explicitly, alongside consonants; there is no inherent vowel in a consonant (abugidas), certain vowels are not systematically dropped (abjads), and consonant and vowel are not combined in the same character (syllabaries).

Of the 441 characters in the Unicode Cyrillic blocks, 177 are historic (33%) and 2 are for Lithuanian dialectology. The remaining 262 are just letters – no punctuation, digits, or combining characters. These are all bicameral, which brings the number of distinct modern letters to 131. Although modern Cyrillic text tends to use precomposed forms, rather than combining diacritics separately with base letters, many extended characters are formed by slightly tweaking a set of basic shapes.

❯ consonantSummary

ConsonantsModern Ukrainian has 22 basic consonant letters (44 if you include both upper- and lowercase), plus the soft sign and the apostrophe representing a hard sign.

Palatalisation can be applied or removed using particular vowel letters, or using the hard and soft signs.

Vowel absence Since this is an alphabet, vowel absence in consonant clusters or after codas is marked simply by an absence of vowel letters. There is no special shaping or mark to indicate a consonant cluster.

Numbers Ukrainian uses ASCII digits.

Layout Ukrainian text runs left to right in horizontal lines. Words are separated by spaces. Letters have an uppercase/lowercase distinction. The shapes of the upper and lowercase forms are typically the same. There can be a significant difference, however, between regular and cursive/italic shapes for the same character.

The visual forms of letters don't usually interact.

Punctuation is ASCII.

Post-consonant vowels

Vowels following consonants are written using 9 vowel letters, 4 of which usually indicate palatalisation of the previous consonant. Palatalisation can otherwise be applied or removed using the hard and soft signs.

Plain vowels

Ukrainian has 5 basic post-consonant vowel sounds in stressed syllables, and 4 more sounds in unstressed syllables. Such allophonic variations are not captured in the orthography. The allocation of letters to sounds also depends on the presence or absence of palatalisation before the vowel.

Here is a list of stressed and unstressed vowel sounds that follow a 'hard' consonant (ie. one that isn't palatalised). The lighter colour transcriptions indicate pronunciation in unstressed syllables.

As post-consonant vowels, the following letters usually indicate that they follow a 'soft' (palatalized) consonant, which should be transcribed as Cʲ.

On the other hand, these letters may also be used after a consonant followed by ʼ (the hard sign), in which case the vowel sound is preceded by j-.

eg.

The vowel letter ї, pronounced ji, is usually only found in standalone position.

Diphthongs

Two consonant characters are used to form diphthongs.

й is occasionally used as a consonant, but is mainly used after a vowel to create diphthongs.

eg.

буйний

в is generally used as a consonant, but when it appears as the coda of a syllable it is pronounced u̯.

eg.

кров

Vowel diacritics

In normal Ukrainian texts there are no combining marks. The following are only for special use.

The position of word stress is contrastive, but not always predictable. ́ is used to indicate where the word stress falls in educational materials, dictionaries, and such.

The other diacritics only appear in decomposed text. 0306 comes from й; 0308 comes from ї.

Standalone vowels

At the start of a word, or after another vowel, Ukrainian just uses ordinary vowel letters. The alternative pronunciations for stressed and unstressed vowels still apply.

Used in these locations, the ioticised vowels are preceded by j-, except for і. Instead, an extra vowel letter ї is used to produce the sound ji. The use of и appears to be very rare.

Vowel absence

Vowel absence principally occurs either when a consonant is a syllable coda, or when a consonant is part of a consonant cluster.

Since this is an alphabet, the absence of vowel sounds in consonant clusters or after codas is marked simply by an absence of vowel letters. There is no special shaping or mark to indicate a consonant cluster.

eg.

селянство

с,е,л,я,н,с,т,в,о

аґрус

а,ґ,р,у,с

This orthography has no special features with respect to the following.

  • Onset clusters.

  • Conjuncts or other shaping related to consonant clusters.
  • Gemination or consonant lengthening.

Ukrainian uses 22 letters (44, if you include uppercase and lowercase) for 24 basic consonant sounds. Most consonant sounds can be palatalised when followed by an ioticised vowel or a soft sign. Palatalised sounds are shown here in a lighter colour. See also palatalisation.

It is worth noting the difference between г, pronounced ɦ in Ukrainian, and ґ, pronounced ɡ.

Observation: Wiktionary IPA transcriptions include sequences such as t͡ʃʲ, ʃt͡ʃʲ, ʃʲ, d͡ʒʲ, which seems odd since these sounds are already palatal. Wikipedia doesn't indicate ʲ for these sounds.

Palatalisation

Most of the consonants can be pronounced with or without palatisation, ie. 'soft' or 'hard', respectively. During palatalisation the tongue is raised towards the palate while the consonant is pronounced, eg. compare

cf.

банан баня

Palatalisation is indicated by the letter that follows the consonant. When the vowel letters ї ю є я follow a consonant, the initial j- sound which is associated with those characters in standalone positions is dropped, and the preceding consonant is palatalised.

When no vowel follows, consonants can be palatalised using the soft sign, ь.

eg.

більшість

The soft sign may also be used when you want the consonant to be palatalised but retain the initial j- of the following vowel.

eg.

досьє

To write a hard consonant followed by a vowel that retains the initial j-, follow the consonant with the hard sign, ʼ.

eg.

пʼять

Note that this shows that there is a difference between пʼят and пять.

A few consonant sounds are generally always hard.

Onsets

Ukrainian syllables can begin with a consonant cluster, but they are just written using the relevant letters.

eg.

друг

пляма

справді

Note that some phonemes are written using a combination of letters.

джгут

Finals

Unlike Russian, voiced consonants at the end of a syllable or word remain voiced.

eg.

шоколад трубкозуб

Other features

Diacritics

0301 is used to indicate where the stress falls in a word for educational materials, dictionaries, and such. The position of the stress is distinctive, and not always predictable, eg. compare

cf.

Text direction

Numbers

Cyrillic uses ASCII digits.

Glyph shaping & positioning

The following features are not found in this orthography.

  • Cursive joining (ie. not joined up like Arabic).
  • Context-based shaping (letters do not interact).
  • Context-based positioning (no combining marks).
  • Context-based positioning for multiple diacritis. (no multiple combining marks).

You can experiment with examples using the All Cyrillic character app and the Ukrainian character app.

Letterform slopes, weights, & italics

Cyrillic doesn't normally have any of the changeability of complex scripts. Characters are typically separate and self-contained. However, there can be a significant difference in shape between regular and italic/cursive font shapes for the same character.

Note in particular the italic form of т in the figure just above, which looks similar to the italic form of м shown in the previous figure.

The shapes of the italic forms can also vary by language.w

The shape of the breve sign in Cyrillic is different from that used for Latin text.s A font such as Brill can detect the appropriate shape from the adjacent characters.

Case & transforms

Ukrainian is bicameral, and applications may need to enable transforms to allow the user to switch between cases.

Baselines, line height, etc.

Cyrillic uses the so-called 'alphabetic' baseline, which is the same as for Latin and many other scripts.

Cyrillic has little in the way of ascenders and descenders, and mostly the font metrics are the same as for ASCII text. One difference is the use of a couple of diacritics, which rise above the ASCII ascender height in capital letters..
