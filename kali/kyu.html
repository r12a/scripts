Usage & history

Kayah Li ( ꤊꤢ꤬ꤛꤢ꤭ꤜꤟꤤ꤬ ꤢ꤬ꤜꤤ꤬ ) is used to write the Eastern and Western Kayah Li languages, spoken by 360,000 and 210,000 people, respectively. Although native to Myanmar, these languages are now also spoken by significant numbers of refugees in Thailand, where the Kayah Li script is taught in schools.

The Kayah Li script was invented in 1962 by Htae Bu Phae (also written Hteh Bu Phe).

Unicode 17 has 1 dedicated Kayah Li block, comprising 48 characters.

More information: Scriptsource • Unicode.

Basic features

The Kayah Li script is an alphabet, ie. all vowels are written explicitly, alongside consonants; there is no inherent vowel in a consonant (abugidas), certain vowels are not systematically dropped (abjads), and consonant and vowel are not combined in the same character (syllabaries).

❯ basicV

Vowels Vowels are written using 4 vowel letters, and 5 more are written using ꤢ and a combining mark

The combining vowel diacritics can also be used with ꤣ to write 5 diphthongs used in loan words. The one common diphthong, ɯə, is somewhat more complicated, however, and uses different sequences of consonant characters to write breathy and non-breathy forms.

Breathy vowels are indicated by preceding them with ꤟ. There is no nasalisation.

Standalone vowels are written using ordinary vowel letters and no special arrangements.

Kayah Li has 3 combining tone marks, which are always used in a straightforward way at the end of every syllable to indicate one of 4 tones. One tone is not marked.

❯ consonantSummary

Consonants Kayah Li has 24 basic consonant letters. Two are not commonly used, and one is used for more than one sound. Otherwise, things are very phonetic.

Vowel absence Since this is an alphabet, vowel absence in consonant clusters or after codas is marked simply by an absence of vowel letters. There is no special shaping or mark to indicate a consonant cluster.

There are no syllable or word final consonants, but syllable-initial clusters may include up to 4 consonants.

NumbersKayah Li has its own set of decimal digits.

Layout Kayah Li text runs left to right in horizontal lines. Words are separated by spaces, and can be multi-syllabic. Letters have no case distinction.

The visual forms of letters don't usually interact.

Punctuation is ASCII, plus one native sentence delimiter.

Notable features

  • half of the plain vowels are represented by letters, and the other half use combining marks over a carrier letter
  • vowel breathiness is written using the letter HA before the vowel
  • vowel marks are always above the base letter, and tone marks always appear below
  • there are no codas in Kayah syllable structures.

Structure

Kayah has 4 syllable types. The CV syllable pattern is the most common.h

V, CV, CCV, CCCV

The syllable-initial consonant clusters involve one or more of the following after the initial: -j -w -r -l

For a list of possible combinations, see onsets.

Tones and breathiness are applied to the syllable nucleus. There are 4 tones.

Kayah words can be 1 – 3 syllables in length, and compound words are relatively common.h

Post-consonant vowels

Four post-consonant vowel sounds are written using letters, and 5 more are written using ꤢ with a combining mark.

The combining vowel diacritics can also be used with ꤣ to write 5 diphthongs used in loan words. The one common diphthong, ɯə, is somewhat more complicated, however, and uses different sequences of consonant characters to write breathy and non-breathy forms.

Breathy vowels are indicated by preceding them with ꤟ. There is no nasalisation.

Diphthongs are mostly represented by a base character and a combining mark. Two diphthongs are represented by a combination of letters. Here is the full set.

Breathiness

Apart from the diphthong just mentioned, breathiness of vowels is indicated by immediately preceding the vowel with ꤟ.

eg.

ꤍꤟꤥ

ꤍ,ꤟ,ꤥ

With a medial consonant, such as -w, the HA continues to be used directly before the vowel.

eg.

ꤗꤝꤟꤤ꤬

ꤗ,ꤝ,ꤟ,ꤤ꤬

However, when ꤛ is the second or third consonant in a cluster, the HA is written before the YA.h

eg.

ꤡꤟꤛꤢ

ꤡ,ꤟ,ꤛ,ꤢ

Vowel length

There appears to be no phonemic distinction in vowel length, and no common way to mark different lengths in the orthography.

However, the punctuation mark ꤮ can be used to indicate extended intonation.

eg.

ꤊꤢ꤬ꤔꤟꤥ꤮ ꤒꤥ꤬

Composites

All but 4 basic vowels in Kayah are represented by multipart vowels made up of a base character and a combining mark. Most of the diphthongs are represented in the same way. Two diphthongs are represented by a combination of letters. Here is the full set.

Vowels may also be mixed with ꤟ to produce breathiness (see breathiness). (The last item in the list above is a special case of that.) All vowels may also be associated with a tone mark (see tones).

Standalone vowels

There appear to be no special mechanisms for dealing with standalone vowels. A written vowel is simply placed where needed.

ꤋꤢ꤭ꤢꤨ꤬

Observation: The Wiktionary entries sometimes show a glottal stop before a standalone vowel, and sometimes do not. See the examples just above.

Observation: The IPA transcriptions for some words found in the Learning Kayah PDF have silent initial vowels, eg. ꤢꤛꤢ꤭ꤞꤤ꤭.

Tones

Kayah Li has four tones, although the high tone is rarely seen. Each syllable is marked for tone, unless it has the mid-high tone. They are written as follows:

low˨ ◌̀ ¹ A92C
high˥ ◌̋ ² A92B
mid˧ ◌̄ ³ A92D
mid-high˦ ◌́ - not written

The following code points are used.

The tone marks all combine below the base (whereas vowel diacritics combine above).r

eg.

ꤞꤢꤨ꤬ ꤞꤢꤨ꤭ ꤞꤢꤨ

The tone mark is normally written as the last component of a syllable, ie. after the vowel.e (Some fonts will correctly display the sequence vowel_base+tone+vowel_diacritic, but in that case the application must be relied upon to treat both orders the same for comparisons.)

See also encoding.

Vowel absence

Vowel absence principally occurs either when a consonant is a syllable coda, or when a consonant is part of a consonant cluster.

Kayah Li syllables have no codas, and the consonant clusters in Kayah are all syllable-initial. Kayah onsets include medial consonants -j- and -w-. When breathiness is introduced to the mix some combinations are a little special. See onsets.

Consonants

This orthography has no special features with respect to the following.

  • Repertoire extensions to cover loan words, etc.
  • Syllable-final consonants.
  • Conjuncts or other shaping related to consonant clusters.

Encoding choices

Visually, two diacritics look as it they could be composed of smaller parts. This section gives guidance on which approach is best.

It is possible that someone may occasionally try to use 2 characters rather than the single character which is canonical. Doing so produces text that will not match correctly encoded text for search, spell-checking, and so on, and so should be avoided. The list below shows the characters in question.

Glyph shaping & positioning

The following features are not found in this orthography.

  • Cursive joining (ie. joined up like Arabic).
  • Context-based shaping (letters do not interact).
  • Case distinction, or special transforms to convert between characters.

Context-based shaping & positioning

Kayah letters don't interact with each other, so no special shaping is needed for that.

Base characters may carry one or two combining marks, and the font needs to correctly shape and position these relative to the base. However, if there are two combining marks, one will appear above and the other below the base, so they won't interfere with each other.
