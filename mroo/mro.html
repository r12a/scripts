Usage & history

Mru (or Mro) is a Tibeto-Burman language spoken primarily in Bangladesh with a few speakers in India. According to the Unicode script proposal, literacy levels for this script among the 100,000 Mro exceed 80%.p Education in the script is available up to grade 3.Scriptsource§https://scriptsource.org/cms/scripts/page.php?item_id=script_detail&key=Mroo

The script was invented by Menlay Murang (or Manley Mro) in the 1980s. Mro folklore says that an earlier script was sent with a cow by the god Turai, but the cow became hungry and ate the book it was carrying. Murang aimed to restore a writing system to the Mro people.

Unicode 17 has 1 dedicated Mro block, comprising 43 characters.

Basic features

The Mro script is an alphabet, ie. all vowels are written explicitly, alongside consonants; there is no inherent vowel in a consonant (abugidas), certain vowels are not systematically dropped (abjads), and consonant and vowel are not combined in the same character (syllabaries).

❯ basicV

Vowels Vowels are written using 7 vowel letters, and one digraph (which combines 2 of the 7).

Mru has no prebase glyphs. or circumgraphs. Multiple vowels may sometimes be used together. There are no letters or diacritics for tone, nasalisation, or vowel length.

Standalone vowels are written using ordinary vowel letters and no special arrangements.

❯ consonantSummary

Consonants Mru has 25 basic consonant letters, but 6 of those are duplicates, representing the same sound as another letter.

Vowel absence Since this is an alphabet, vowel absence in consonant clusters or after codas is marked simply by an absence of vowel letters. There are no combining marks or special forms for consonant clusters. Syllable medial and final consonants are represented using ordinary consonant letters.

NumbersMro has its own set of digits.

Layout Mru text runs left to right in horizontal lines. Words are separated by spaces. Letters have no case distinction.

Line-breaking and justification are primarily based on inter-word spaces.

Punctuation is mostly that used for the Latin script, but Mro includes 2 native punctuation marks resembling dandas.

Vowels

This orthography has no special features with respect to the following.

  • Consonants used to create vowel sounds.
  • Pre-base vowels or circumgraphs, or multipart vowels.
  • Vowel length.
  • Nasalisation (not a feature of the language).
  • Tones (not a tonal language).
  • Standalone vowels.
  • Consonants that are not followed by a vowel.
 

Post-consonant vowels

Mro doesn't indicate tones, nasalisation or long vowels.

No special mechanisms or rules are employed to indicate consonants that are not followed by a vowel. The vowel letter is simply omitted.

Standalone vowels

Standalone vowel sounds are simply represented using ordinary vowel letters.

Vowel absence

Vowel absence principally occurs either when a consonant is a syllable coda, or when a consonant is part of a consonant cluster.

Since this is an alphabet, the absence of vowel sounds in consonant clusters or after codas is marked simply by an absence of vowel letters. There is no special shaping or mark to indicate a consonant cluster.

eg.

𖩄𖩑𖩁𖩌𖩑

𖩄,𖩑,𖩁,𖩌,𖩑

𖩃𖩊𖩏

𖩃,𖩊,𖩏

Mru has no conjunct forms for consonant clusters.

Consonants

This orthography has no special features with respect to the following.

  • Repertoire extensions to cover loan words, etc.
  • Onset clusters.
  • Syllable-final consonants.
  • Conjuncts or other shaping related to consonant clusters.
  • Gemination or consonant lengthening.

Numbers, dates, currency, etc

Digits

Mru has its own set of digits, with a decimal base.

Text direction

Mru text is written horizontally, with lines that flow from top to bottom.

Glyph shaping & positioning

The following features are not found in this orthography.

  • Cursive joining (ie. joined up like Arabic).
  • Context-based shaping (letters do not interact).
  • Context-based positioning (no combining marks).
  • Context-based positioning for multiple diacritis. (no multiple combining marks).
  • Case distinction, or special transforms to convert between characters.
